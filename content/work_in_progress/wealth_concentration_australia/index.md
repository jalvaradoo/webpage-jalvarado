Wealth has become more concentrated at the top in many rich countries since the 1970s. Three explanations compete: lower taxes on the rich, more unequal earnings at the top, and higher returns on the assets the rich hold. This paper studies what has driven wealth concentration in Australia since 1970. I use a version of the model of Hubmer, Krusell and Smith (2021), calibrated to Australia in 1970 and fed with the measured paths of four forces: taxes, earnings dispersion, top earnings and asset returns. The main result is that taxes and top earnings together explain most of the rise in the top 1% wealth share in the model, from 8.4% in 1970 to 14.0% in 2020. Taxes are the largest single force: on their own, they account for 43% of the rise.
