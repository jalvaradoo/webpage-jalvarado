This paper asks what has driven wealth concentration in Australia since 1970. I use a quantitative heterogeneous-agent model calibrated to Australia in 1970, and feed it the measured paths from 1970 to 2025 of four forces: taxes, earnings dispersion, top earnings and the returns on wealth, with the world real interest rate as a fifth force in an open-economy version. I measure how much of the change in top wealth shares each force accounts for, evaluate major Australian tax reforms, from the 1985-88 tax package to the 2027 replacement of the capital gains discount, and ask why wealth is less concentrated in Australia than in the United States.
