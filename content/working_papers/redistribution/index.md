Countries with similar income inequality before taxes and transfers redistribute very differently. To explain these differences, I develop a heterogeneous-agent general-equilibrium model in which a decisive voter chooses tax progressivity. A parameter, the wealth bias of political influence, sets the voter's position in the wealth distribution. Data on the political influence of wealth accounts for 45% of the variance of the recovered bias. Under the average bias of the four largest Nordic countries, Mexico, with its own earnings dispersion and government size, would lower its income Gini by 39% instead of 2%. Restoring one person one vote would raise average welfare in 34 of 37 countries, by up to 3.1% of consumption; in Chile, the poorest fifth of households by wealth would gain 11% and the richest fifth would lose 2%.
