Countries with similar income inequality before taxes and transfers redistribute very differently. To explain these differences, I develop a heterogeneous-agent general-equilibrium model in which a decisive voter chooses tax progressivity. A parameter, the wealth bias of political influence, sets the voter's position in the wealth distribution. Holding preferences and technology common across countries, differences in the wealth bias account for the differences in how much taxes and transfers lower the income Gini. The implied bias is consistent with data on the political influence of wealth. Removing the bias would raise average welfare by up to 3.1% of consumption.
